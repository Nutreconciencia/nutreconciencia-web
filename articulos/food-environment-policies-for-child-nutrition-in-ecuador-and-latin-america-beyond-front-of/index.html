Food-environment policies for child nutrition in Ecuador and Latin America: beyond front-of-package labels and advertising restrictions.

José Francisco López-Gil, Fiorella Quiroz-Cárdenas, Miguel López-Moreno

Resumen científico

Lo esencial en menos de un minuto.

Drawing on evidence from Ecuador and comparable middle-income settings, we argue that labeling and advertising policies are necessary but insufficient when healthier options remain unavailable, unaffordable, or outcompeted at point of sale. We outline complementary food-environment actions (e.g., school food environments, fiscal incentives for minimally processed foods, digital marketing regulation, and alignment with local food systems) within a framework that addresses commercial determinants of health and governance safeguards against industry capture.

QUÉ ABORDA

¿Qué cuestión aborda?

Latin American countries have expanded front-of-package warning labels and restrictions on food marketing to children, yet childhood overweight and obesity continue to rise. Ultra-processed foods (UPFs) dominate retail and digital environments and remain embedded in school settings.

Qué significa
